---
title: How a Heat Pump Works
subtitle: Moving heat instead of making it
---

## [hook] More heat than you pay for
A heat pump can deliver three or four units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses. That sounds like it breaks physics. It doesn't, because a heat pump doesn't make heat. It moves it.

## [idea] Pumping heat uphill
Left alone, heat always flows from hot to cold. But even winter air holds plenty of thermal energy. A heat pump spends a little work to push heat the other way, from the cold outdoors into a warm home, like a pump lifting water uphill.

## [parts] Four key parts
The carrier is a refrigerant, a fluid that boils at very low temperatures. It travels in a sealed loop through four parts: an evaporator, a compressor, a condenser, and an expansion valve.

## [outside] Absorb and compress
Outside, cold liquid refrigerant flows through the evaporator coil. It's colder than the outdoor air, so it soaks up heat and boils into a gas. Then the compressor squeezes that gas. Its pressure rises, and so does its temperature, well above room temperature.

## [inside] Release and expand
Indoors, the hot gas flows through the condenser. A fan blows room air across the coil, the refrigerant gives up its heat, and it condenses back into a liquid. Finally, the expansion valve drops the pressure, the refrigerant turns cold again, and the loop starts over.

## [cop] Coefficient of performance
Because the electricity only moves heat, a typical heat pump delivers about three units of heat per unit of electricity. Engineers call this the coefficient of performance. A simple electric heater manages just one. So on a mild day, roughly two thirds of the heat in your home was pulled, for free, out of the air outside.

## [reverse] Run it backwards
Add a reversing valve, and the refrigerant flows the other way. Now the indoor coil absorbs heat, and the outdoor coil dumps it outside. The same machine becomes an air conditioner, keeping you warm in winter and cool in summer.

## [recap] Recap
So a heat pump is a heat mover. Boil, compress, condense, expand, and repeat, carrying warmth to wherever you want it.
